# Reflector

## One-page overview

In Human Design, the Reflector is associated with openness, sensitivity, environmental awareness, and reflecting the health of a community. Reflectors are traditionally described as having no consistently defined centers, making their experience especially responsive to people, places, and timing.

This openness is not presented as a weakness. It can offer a wide range of perception and a nuanced ability to sense whether an environment is healthy, coherent, or out of balance.

## Strategy: wait through a lunar cycle

For major decisions, Reflectors are traditionally encouraged to observe their experience across a full lunar cycle—approximately 28 days—before committing.

The purpose is not to search for one permanently stable feeling. It is to hear the changing perspectives that emerge as the Reflector moves through different days, people, and environments.

## Healthy and strained expressions

The traditional **signature** is **surprise**: a sense of freshness, wonder, and discovery in the experience of life.

The traditional **not-self theme** is **disappointment**. Disappointment may arise when an environment, community, or expectation does not support the Reflector’s openness and sensitivity.

Disappointment can become a question: “Is this truly my experience, or am I reflecting the condition of this place or group?”

## In work and relationships

Reflectors may benefit greatly from choosing environments carefully. The quality of a workplace, home, or community may affect them as much as the formal role or activity itself.

Relationships can be supportive when there is room for changing perspectives rather than pressure to be consistent in every moment. A Reflector’s changing response need not be treated as unreliability.

## A practical experiment

For a significant decision, keep a simple lunar-cycle journal. Record how the decision feels in different environments and with different people. Look for recurring clarity rather than demanding a single constant emotional state.
